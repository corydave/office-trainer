How it works

  1. Learn one idea

    A short lesson explains one skill, with the exact menu path and a quick question to check you've got it.

  2. Practice in a real file

    Open the practice file in Word, Excel or PowerPoint and do the steps any way you like: the Ribbon, a shortcut or the right-click menu all count.

  3. Check your work

    The trainer looks at the result, not your clicks, and tells you what's right and what to fix. Inside the app, it can also show you where.

  4. Earn badges and certificates

    Every drill, skill check and project earns a badge. Finish a lesson for a certificate that lists the skills you showed.

Inside the app (the sidebar)

Add Office Trainer to Word, Excel or PowerPoint once, and it opens as a panel next to your file. It reads the file you have open, so checking takes one click.

Works in the web apps (including on a Chromebook) and in the desktop apps on Windows and Mac. See Install.

In any browser

No add-in? Open a course from this page, download each practice file, do the work in any version of the app (even on a phone), save it, and select Upload my file and check. The file opens in your browser tab to be checked; it isn't sent anywhere.

Made for every learner

  • Ready for the exam. The lessons cover every skill on the Microsoft Office Specialist Associate exams (MO-110, MO-210, MO-310), and each course ends with a timed practice exam.
  • Real skills, not trivia. Projects are things you'd actually make: a budget, a cover letter, a team presentation.
  • Practice at your own pace. Skill checks can be untimed or given extra time.
  • Readable your way. Pick your fonts, such as Atkinson Hyperlegible, Verdana or OpenDyslexic (if it's installed). Light and dark modes follow your system.
  • Keyboard friendly. Everything works without a mouse. F6 (Ctrl+F6 in the web apps) moves between your file, the Ribbon and the trainer.
  • Accessible by design. Lessons teach alt text, contrast and reading order as you go, and videos are published only with checked captions.

Add it to Word, Excel or PowerPoint

One add-in works in all three apps. Install it once and it shows the right course in each one.

Install it yourself

  1. Download manifest.xml (the small file that tells the app where the trainer lives).
  2. Open a blank document, workbook or presentation in Word, Excel or PowerPoint on the web (office.com).
  3. On the Home tab, select Add-ins, then More Settings (older menus say More Add-ins or Advanced), then Upload My Add-in. The menu moves now and then; it's always under Add-ins.
  4. Browse to manifest.xml and select Upload. An Office Trainer button appears on the Home tab.

If you clear your browser data or switch browsers, upload it again. Some schools turn off uploading add-ins; if the option isn't there, ask your IT office.

For the desktop apps, or for a whole class, ask your school's Microsoft 365 administrator to install it for you.

Install it for a whole school

A Microsoft 365 administrator can give it to everyone at once, so nobody uploads anything: Microsoft 365 admin center > Settings > Integrated apps > Upload custom apps, choose Office Add-in as the app type, upload manifest.xml and pick who gets it. It can take up to a day to appear.

The add-in asks only for access to the file that's open, so it can read it for checking and open practice files. It can't see anything else on your computer or account.

Your privacy

Office Trainer was built to need nothing from you.

  • Your file stays on your device. The checker runs inside the trainer panel (or this browser tab). Your document is never uploaded or stored anywhere.
  • No accounts. There's nothing to sign up for. Your progress, badges and certificates are saved only in your own browser, and you can save a copy of your record as a file.
  • No tracking. No analytics, no ads and no cookies. Fonts are hosted here, so no font service learns you visited.
  • Videos in privacy-enhanced mode. Lesson videos come only from approved hosts, and YouTube videos use its privacy-enhanced mode, which doesn't set tracking cookies until you press play.
  • Practice files are made up. The lessons follow a fictional company, and remind you to keep real names, grades and account numbers out of your projects.

In a class section (planned), the results your instructor needs will go only to your instructor. Until then, every course runs in practice mode: nothing leaves your device.

Plan with the course maps

Each map shows the course outcomes, outline topics, weekly objectives, every drill and its exam skills, the projects with their rubrics, and how each outcome is assessed.

Adapt the lessons

Lessons are plain Markdown files with a few simple blocks for drills, quizzes and checks. Edit them in the lesson editor, which checks your changes and previews them as students will see them. Changes go live as soon as the file is uploaded, with nothing for students to reinstall.

Each course also has a checker test page (for example PowerPoint checker tests) that runs every check against the answer keys.
